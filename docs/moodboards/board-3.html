What we took, and why.

Partiful / invitation maker

The invitation is the object people share. Give each card a recognizable cover, then keep its destination obvious. Borrow the social artifact, not a copy of its artwork.

Amie / designing joyful productivity

Components before screens, focus before feature volume. Make the mundane row and the empty state pleasant enough to keep.

Nate Parrott / Arc artifacts

His mobile Spaces, referral ticket and share sheet show how a utilitarian object can have character. Borrow the compact collectible shape, not browser chrome.

Airbnb / 2025 summer release

A richer product can still scan quickly. Distinct objects and a clear profile make broad features approachable; avoid adding another navigation layer.

Jakub Krehel / interface details

Balanced titles, tabular scores, and a faint outline on images. Small typographic corrections compound faster than another gradient.

Emil Kowalski / 7 practical animation tips

Press to 0.97, enter from 0.96 rather than zero, and let ease-out do the work. Feedback is immediate; movement ends in 240ms.

Family values

Simplicity, fluidity, delight. Reveal scoring in a tray only when someone asks for it; never make the draft feel like a settings page.

Cash App / color guidelines

Use a recognizable hue with discipline. Large amounts and a single action beat coloring every financial status green.

What earns its place

Keep the warmth and the idea of a keepsake. This direction is strongest for fantasy draft day and a season recap, where an artifact is more interesting than an app screen.

What stays out

Do not replace SF throughout the app with a serif. It would conflict with GAME-UI and Peyton's existing pages. Avoid faux texture downloads, distressed typography, and ticket stubs that shrink touch targets.
